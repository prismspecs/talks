## Overview

Grayson Earle, former Pioneer Works resident, presents a toolkit for workers striking or unionizing. The talk frames "post-awareness art" — moving past producing evidence of broken systems toward intervening on the systems themselves — through a series of projects and historical precedents.

---

## Workshop: Strike — Speculative Tools for Workplace Protest

Participants gather (in person or Zoom) to discuss workplace problems — wage transparency, pay discrepancies, inability to unionize — then invent tools to address them. Some projects are speculative; some are very real.

**Favorite idea: Printer Hack.** Hacking office printers so that before any print job, a sheet of organizing material comes out first ("Meet at the lunch room at 2:00 PM to plan our union") — or just solid black pages to burn through company ink.

Also includes performance interventions, including a collaboration that hacked a Hans Haacke museum exhibit.

## Post-Awareness Art

> "We are at peak awareness. Everyone more or less knows what's going on. The real challenge is how to move beyond that and intervene directly on the systems themselves rather than produce more evidence of why the system is so messed up."

## FloodNet — Electronic Disturbance Theater (Ricardo Dominguez, 1998)

A program you download that makes your computer part of a DDoS attack — sending millions of low-level pings to overwhelm a server. (Same technique Anonymous used against payment processors blocking WikiLeaks donations.)

**Legal precedent:** After launching FloodNet against the World Economic Forum, Dominguez successfully argued in court that it was protected as free speech — both as an artwork and as a **virtual sit-in**, placing it in the lineage of 1960s sit-ins. This framing simultaneously protects it legally and grants it historical continuity with political resistance.

## Etoy War (late 1990s)

The art collective Etoy held the domain `etoy.com`. A dot-com-era corporation ("Etoy Incorporated") launched a cease-and-desist, demanding they surrender it.

The collective refused and launched a **gamified campaign**: participants earned points for escalating media coverage of the dispute — 10 points for a negative online review, 100 for local news, 10,000 for national coverage. The corporation's stock price eventually went to zero. (Coincided with the dot-com crash, but the campaign contributed.)

## SETI@home & Folding@home

**SETI@home:** Berkeley's SETI arm couldn't afford supercomputer time to crunch satellite data from the Puerto Rico array. Solution: distribute raw data to volunteers' computers. 10,000 idle machines together outperform a supercomputer. Created an ad hoc distributed supercomputer — and it worked.

> "It's not really like volunteering or donating. It's another step. You are doing the thing itself as well with them. It transforms you into a more invested participant."

---

## Bail Block (Grayson Earle)

Inspired by SETI@home. A program that runs in the background and **mines Monero** (cryptocurrency), using roughly the resources of a Netflix stream. The mined crypto is converted to USD and used to bail people out of pre-trial detention.

Partnered with the **Connecticut Immigrant Bond Fund** (previously the Bronx Freedom Fund) to release people detained by ICE.

---

## Protest Cube / Reflecto Cube — Tools for Action

An inflatable transparent cube that acts as a **physical barrier between protesters and police** — protecting against batons. Re-inflatable, reusable in times of crisis. Described as "both aesthetically beautiful and serves a purpose for political engagement."

## Themes

- **Post-awareness:** Stop documenting injustice; start intervening on the systems that produce it.
- **Distributed participation:** Not charity or volunteering — contributing computing power to *do the thing itself* (DDoS, crypto mining, data crunching).
- **Legal framing as artistic strategy:** The "virtual sit-in" precedent protects digital protest by grounding it in civil rights lineage.
- **Gamification of dissent:** Etoy's point system turns spectatorship into collective action.
- **Speculative tools with real teeth:** From printer hacks to crypto-for-bail pipelines — the line between concept and function is deliberately blurred.
