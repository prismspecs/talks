bail is "money or some form of property that is deposited to the court by the suspect, in return for the release from pre-trial detention. If the suspect does not return to court, the bail is forfeited, and the suspect may possibly be brought up on charges of the crime of failure to appear."

In some ways I can see how this is a rational design, you want to make sure that people don't use bail as a means of skipping town and accountability for a crime. But of course the obvious problem is that anyone with wealth gets to remain free before their court date while anyone without is put behind bars. This has a vastly disproportionate effect on low income people and communities of color. In communities with impacted court systems like New York City, the pre-trial period can last months or even years. Kalief Browder, a young black kid from the Bronx, was accused of stealing a backpack and spent three years on Riker's Island awaiting trial before taking his own life.

Even if the pre-trial period is set for a period of a few weeks, it still impacts one's chances at avoiding the prison system. A New York Times op Ed entitled the Baile trap from a few years ago cited research that indicated that people who were allowed to remain free during their pre-trial period were much more likely to be found innocent. Time spent with family and the ability to more calmly strategize help in this regard.

Nation wide, the conviction rate for people not detained for pretrial: 50%

For those who are detained during pretrial: 92%

It's important to remember that categorically speaking, anyone elligible for bail has not yet been found guilty of anything.

What tends to happen given all of the factors that have just reviewed his people who are unable to meet Bail are offered plea-bargain's. Plea-bargain is when the district attorney offers you the chance to reduce a jail sentence or avoid it altogether in return for a guilty plea. So a lot of people will take this, even though it means they will not have the rights to us three and fair trial met by the state. In New York State, 90% of people met with this decision opt for a plea bargain. That means they forfeit their constitutional right to be tried before a jury, are never allowed to argue their case, and can never be found innocent. Put simply, these people are found guilty of poverty.

Even though 90% of people who cant Ford Baile take plea-bargain's, the courts are still so impacted the judges your cases as late as midnight on Friday night. If twice as many people were able to afford bail, the justice system would collapse under its own weight. Another way of thinking about this is that the justice system in America makes as a basic assumption that poor people will not have their day in court.

It also opens up an attack vector, and an interesting question: what would happen if everyone in america was able to pay bail?

## Slide 40 · Hans Haacke’s MoMA poll {#s40}

Hans Haacke is a German-born artist who lives and works in New York City. Haacke is considered a "leading exponent" of Institutional Critique.

In 1970 Hans Haacke proposed a work for the exhibition entitled Information to be held at the Museum of Modern Art in New York (an exhibition meant to be an overview of current younger artists), according to which the visitors would be asked to vote on a current socio-political issue. The proposal was accepted, and Haacke prepared his installation, entitled MoMA Poll, but did not hand in the specific question until right before the opening of the show. His query asked, "Would the fact that Governor Rockefeller has not denounced President Nixon's Indochina Policy be a reason for your not voting for him in November?" Visitors were asked to deposit their answers in the appropriate one of two transparent Plexiglas ballot boxes. At the end of the exhibition, there were approximately twice as many Yes ballots as No ballots. Haacke's question commented directly on the involvements of a major donor and board member at MOMA, Nelson Rockefeller. This installation is an early example of what in the art world came to be known as institutional critique.

## Slide 58 · Usefulness of a Useless Neural Network {#s58}

In this exploration of machine learning, I focus on the architecture of neural networks as I create a model in real time, speaking about the process throughout. Machine learning, celebrated for its novel approach to teaching machines to think like humans, involves training neural networks on massive datasets until accurate predictions for specific tasks are achieved. This experimental lecture emphasizes the optimization process, tuning systems for improved results over time.

Using a metaphor of a river, I explain how neural networks flow in one direction with various obstructions shaping their path. The training process involves adjusting these obstructions to achieve the desired flow. A conversation with JFI computer science researcher Francis Tseng during the performance introduces technical aspects like weights, biases, and back propagation, providing insights into how neural networks are fine-tuned for accuracy.

## Slide 59 · Usefulness of a Useless Neural Network {#s59}

The video also explores the practicality of optimization, highlighting the trade-offs in minimizing maps for navigators, using Borges’ On Exactitude in Science as a guiding light. It delves into the fundamental problem of adding two numbers, abstracted to a machine learning context. The explanation of a neural network's structure, training process, and the analogy of a river contribute to my desire to teach people about how these things actually work under the hood.

It wraps up with an example of a well-trained neural network adding two numbers together and introduces the optimization challenge of reducing large datasets to interconnected neurons. As you can see, a predictive model is just that, predictive, producing a projection of 1 + 2 = 3.09.

## Slide 63 · Inference Engine at Enter the Neuroverse festival in New York City {#s63}

In Inference Engine, the player is positioned inside the latent space of a Generative Adversarial Network, a common network infrastructure used to generate photorealistic images, pre-dating guided diffusion based models like Dall-E and MidJourney. The player must traverse this space by controlling the levers of a hyperdimensional spaceship, thus manipulating the neurons in the network by hand, achieving a successful navigation of this complex system.

---

## Slide 64 · Navigating from one latent space coordinate to another - Inference Engine {#s64}

A GAN is a a model which takes in an arbitrary set of values, in this case 1024 numbers, and outputs an image. You can think of the input numbers as a sort of address, in the sense that if you input the same set of numbers twice you get the same output. Slightly change one number, and you’ll end up not far away from your previous location. This project turns that into a game mechanic, prompting the player to navigate between these addresses in order to find a destination within the predictive model. You can see the landscape changing as the player navigates the latent space here.

## Slide 66 {#s66}

Scrivener is a text-based game that takes place on Discord, drawing inspiration from Dungeons & Dragons (DnD), Decentralized Autonomous Organizations (DAOs), and utilizing an AI-assisted storyteller to create an expansive future-building game. The game is set in a near-future world where illiberalism has gained a strong foothold, and the last bastions of the left have united to form a DAO to challenge the new world order. Players from various leftist factions must collaborate to propose and build a new world, responding to crises and engaging in prefigurative actions. Players can also propose to change the rules of the game as it progresses.

The first play through happened as part of Goldsmith University’s Weird Economies summer program. The events in the game were inspired by the topics covered during the biweekly lectures. ## Slide 68 · FACTIONS {#s68}

Players will join one of several factions (“the last bastions of the left”), each with its own unique perspective and approach to resisting the new world order. Factions include Forever Marxists, Progressive Deep State, Anarcho-Syndicalists, Beyond-Us Monadists, and Postcapitalist Eschatologists. These are intended to be fun but also framing devices for the sorts of political actions that might be dreamed up during the course of play. The factions are also AI-assisted, so you can ask them questions about what to do in any given scenario and they will give you some ideas based on their political ideology.
